Logo guidelines

The following guidance starts with the simplest way to use our logo, then goes into more detail for designers.

It covers:

  • Our main logo
  • Our full-name logo
  • Getting the right logo for your project
  • Single-colour versions
  • Alternative formats
  • Background colours our logo can be paired with
  • How to maintain clear space round the logo
  • Positioning the logo

Our main logo

Our horizontal, full-colour logo works for most use cases. It’s available in different versions suitable for dark backgrounds and light backgrounds. 

Information alert banner.

Only for use on flagship or permanent projects

Any projects celebrating our bicentenary or promoting related events should

use our UCL200 logo.

Full-name logo

Research has shown us that the acronym “UCL” is not always recognisable in some parts of the world. The full-name version of our logo should therefore be used for audiences less familiar with UCL, such as prospective international students or staff.

Furthermore, the phrase “University College London” spelled out in full must appear somewhere in the layout of all external communications. If our full name does not appear elsewhere already, you should use this version of our logo.

Need the logo? Get in touch.

Get the right logo for your project by contacting the brand team with your requirements.

Design guidelines 

Here’s how to use our logo in your designs. 

Single-colour versions

A single-colour, dark purple version is also available, for when the full-colour logo is impractical or no longer accessible on a coloured background.

When colour reproduction is not possible, use the black version or the white version.

Back to top ↑

Alternative formats

Our logo also comes in different formats, for use in exceptional circumstances or when horizontal space is limited. These formats are only available for use with permission from the brand team. 

Colour pairings  

For the sake of accessibility, our logo should only be paired with the following background colours. 

Full-colour logo

Full-colour logos can only be placed on white or dark purple backgrounds. These versions provide the clearest expression of our brand and should be used wherever possible. 

Single-colour logo

Single-colour logos can be used more flexibly across a wider range of background colours, provided there is sufficient contrast and legibility.

Back to top ↑ 

Maintaining clear space round the logo 

Clear space is defined by the width of the stem of the L. No other elements should enter this area.

The clear space can extend beyond the edge of a page and does not need to align with page margins. Its role is simply to protect the logo from crowding.

Back to top ↑

Positioning the logo 

When the logo appears alongside other content, such as text, images, or graphics, it should sit in one of the four corners of the layout.

It can be used in any corner, depending on space and composition.  

When the logo stands alone, for example on intro or end frames, holding slides, or screens with no other content, it may be placed centrally within the space.

For web layouts and digital interfaces, the logo should be positioned according to the rules of the UCL design system.
